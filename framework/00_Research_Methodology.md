# Automotive Consultation Framework (ACF)

## Research Methodology

Version: 0.1

---

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this research is to determine whether a structured Automotive Consultation Framework (ACF) is needed, and if so, to design it using evidence, existing knowledge, and systematic validation rather than assumptions.

The research will identify existing approaches, evaluate their strengths and limitations, discover gaps, and develop a technology-independent methodology for automotive consultation.

---

## 2. Research Objectives

The research seeks to answer the following questions:

1. What consultation approaches already exist?

2. What methodologies are currently used for automotive decision making?

3. What standards or frameworks are relevant?

4. What problems remain unsolved?

5. Can a structured consultation methodology improve automotive decision quality?

6. What should such a methodology contain?

---

## 3. Research Principles

The research will follow these principles:

• Evidence before implementation

• Research before conclusions

• Credit existing work

• Avoid unsupported claims

• Challenge assumptions

• Validate every important principle

• Technology independence

• Customer-centric thinking

---

## 5. Evidence Sources

Research will consider:

Academic literature

Industry standards

Automotive organizations

Professional publications

Conference papers

Research journals

Books

Automotive experts

Public technical documentation

Publicly available software

Professional communities

---

## 6. Inclusion Criteria

Include sources that:

Describe consultation

Describe decision support

Describe customer buying processes

Describe automotive purchasing

Describe explainable AI

Describe structured methodologies

Describe customer decision frameworks

---

## 7. Exclusion Criteria

Exclude sources that:

Only review vehicles

Only advertise products

Lack sufficient technical detail

Do not contribute to consultation methodology

---

## 8. Limitations

The research is based upon publicly available information.

Private organizational methodologies, confidential processes and unpublished work cannot be evaluated.

Therefore, conclusions will always be stated as:

"Based on publicly available evidence..."

rather than absolute claims.

---

## 9. Success Criteria

The research will be considered successful if it produces:

• A validated problem statement

• A documented literature review

• A clear gap analysis

• A well-defined consultation methodology

• A framework that can be implemented by software while remaining independent of any specific technology.

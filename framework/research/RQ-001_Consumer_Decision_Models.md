# 1. Research Question

How do people make important purchasing decisions, and what lessons can be applied to the Automotive Consultation Framework (ACF)?

---

# 2. Objective

The objective of this research is to understand established consumer decision-making models and identify concepts that may be relevant when designing the Automotive Consultation Framework (ACF).

This research focuses on general consumer purchasing behaviour rather than automotive-specific buying processes.

---

# 3. Scope

This research includes:

- Consumer purchasing behaviour
- Consumer decision-making models
- Information gathering behaviour
- Alternative evaluation
- Purchase decision process
- Decision confidence
- Information overload

This research does not include:

- Automotive dealership processes
- Vehicle recommendation systems
- Vehicle comparison websites
- Automotive consultation methodologies

Those topics will be covered in later research questions.

# 6. Common Evidence

The reviewed sources consistently indicate the following:

- Purchasing is generally a structured process rather than a single event.
- Consumers first recognize a need before searching for information.
- Consumers actively gather information from multiple sources.
- Consumers compare available alternatives before making a purchase.
- Purchasing decisions are influenced by multiple evaluation criteria rather than a single factor.
- Consumers often evaluate their decision after the purchase.
- Excessive information can reduce decision quality by increasing cognitive overload.
- Decision confidence plays an important role in customer satisfaction and purchasing decisions.

---

# 7. Research Summary

The reviewed literature consistently describes purchasing as a structured decision-making process that begins with recognizing a need and progresses through information gathering, alternative evaluation, purchase and post-purchase evaluation.

Consumer behaviour research also indicates that modern consumers are exposed to large amounts of information from multiple sources. While information supports decision-making, excessive information may increase cognitive overload, making purchasing decisions more difficult.

The reviewed literature further suggests that customer confidence is an important factor during purchasing decisions and is influenced by both the quality and organization of available information.

These findings provide valuable background knowledge for the Automotive Consultation Framework (ACF). However, the reviewed literature primarily describes consumer purchasing behaviour rather than prescribing how a professional automotive consultation should be conducted.

---

# 8. ACF Decisions

Based on the reviewed evidence, the following framework decisions are adopted for ACF.

---

## AD-001

A consultation shall be treated as a structured process rather than a single recommendation.

---

## AD-002

The consultation should reduce information overload by organizing and filtering relevant information.

---

## AD-003

The consultation should improve customer confidence through transparent reasoning and explainable recommendations.

# 10. Conclusion

Consumer decision-making research provides valuable insight into how customers approach important purchasing decisions.

The reviewed literature establishes a strong foundation for understanding consumer behaviour but does not define a structured methodology for conducting automotive consultations.

These findings will inform the design of the Automotive Consultation Framework (ACF) while automotive-specific consultation practices will be examined separately in subsequent research activities.

# Automotive Consulting Framework (ACF)

# Chapter 4 – Consultation Methodology

## Purpose

This chapter defines how each consultation lifecycle phase is executed.

Rather than prescribing a fixed sequence of questions, ACF defines the objectives, activities, decision-making approach, and quality expectations for each phase. This allows both human consultants and AI implementations to conduct natural consultations while maintaining methodological consistency.

---

# 4.1 Discover

## Purpose

The purpose of the Discover phase is to build a comprehensive understanding of the customer before attempting diagnosis or vehicle recommendations.

The consultant should focus on understanding the customer's situation, objectives, constraints, preferences, and intended usage through a natural conversation rather than a rigid questionnaire.

---

## Information Prioritization Model

The purpose of the Discover phase is not to collect every possible piece of customer information. Instead, it is to progressively build sufficient understanding to enable accurate diagnosis and confident recommendations.

Therefore, information should be gathered according to its contribution to the customer's purchasing decision rather than in a fixed sequence.

The consultant should continuously identify the most valuable missing information and naturally guide the conversation toward it.

---

## Information Categories

Rather than treating all information equally, ACF classifies customer information into three categories.

### Critical Information

Critical information has a direct impact on vehicle suitability and must be understood before recommendations can be made.

Examples include:

- Intended vehicle usage
- Budget
- Passenger capacity
- Vehicle category or body style
- Annual running
- Planned ownership duration

---

### Important Information

Important information significantly improves recommendation quality but may be collected later in the consultation.

Examples include:

- Fuel preference
- Transmission preference
- Driving environment
- Parking constraints
- Technology expectations

---

### Optional Information

Optional information improves personalization but is not normally required to produce accurate recommendations.

Examples include:

- Preferred colour
- Interior preferences
- Passenger composition (adults versus children)
- Brand familiarity
- Lifestyle preferences

---

## Dynamic Consultation Principle

The consultation should not follow a rigid questionnaire.

After every customer response, the consultant should determine:

> **What is the most valuable missing information at this moment?**

The next question should maximize customer understanding while minimizing unnecessary questioning.

---

## ACF Principle

The framework defines information priorities rather than fixed questions.

This enables different consultants, organizations, and AI implementations to conduct natural conversations while maintaining methodological consistency.
